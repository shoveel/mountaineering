# Field Survey

**A visual philosophy of patient measurement.**

Field Survey treats every surface as a page torn from an expedition notebook that a cartographer, a naturalist and a draughtsman kept together. Space is organised the way terrain is surveyed: by strata, contour and elevation. Forms are stacked in bands, never floated; a composition rises from a heavy, dark base toward a thin, bright summit, so the eye always climbs. Emptiness is not leftover space but sky, and it is given generously, because the eye must have somewhere to breathe before the next ascent.

Colour is drawn from altitude itself. A deep glacier ink anchors the ground; worn paper and snow carry the light; one ember of alpenglow orange is reserved for what matters most: a reading, an arrow, a single annotated word. Greens and greys appear only as the muted tones of forest, lichen and rock, laid in flat, quiet fields. Nothing is saturated for effect. The palette is calibrated so carefully that a single orange mark carries the weight of a headline: the product of painstaking chromatic restraint.

Information is shown as an instrument would show it. Density of marks stands for quantity: dots thin out as air thins, hatching thickens where danger lives, ticks march up an axis like footsteps. Leader lines run with surveyor's precision from a specimen to its note. Annotations are handwritten, slightly imperfect and alive, set against strict mono readouts and a monumental signage typeface, so the human hand and the measuring device always sit in dialogue on the same page.

Typography is architecture, not paragraph. A sturdy, carved display face names things the way a park sign names a trail; a quiet mono face records numbers; a hurried hand-lettered script adds the field note. Words are few and placed only where the drawing cannot speak alone. Every label is aligned to a shared baseline grid, and every line weight is deliberate, the result of countless refinements by someone at the top of their craft.

The finished work must look labored over: master-level execution in which every ridge vertex, tick spacing and margin has been measured and reconsidered. Repetition is used with patience, so that a field of a thousand small marks resolves into one clear idea. The final plate should feel like evidence: proof that something vast and fragile can be mapped, understood and protected through careful, meticulous attention.
